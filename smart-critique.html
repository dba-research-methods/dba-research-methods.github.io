Research Methods Ledger · 2026 Edition

SMART is a framework. The dogma is the problem.

SMART objectives are the default way doctoral candidates are taught to frame research aims — and for good reason. But a framework is a tool, not a constitution. This ledger lays out what SMART does well, the first-order critiques of its criteria, and the deeper second-order challenges that emerge when the framework is institutionalised and followed dogmatically in DBA research.

Field note: a framework you follow without thinking stops being a method and becomes a ritual. Use SMART — don't worship it.

01 · The framework

Five criteria for a research objective

SMART is a mnemonic for framing aims and objectives. In DBA research it is the standard scaffold for turning a vague ambition into a defensible research contract. Used well, it forces precision early.

S

Specific

A focused research aim — the phenomenon, the unit of analysis, and the context named precisely.

M

Measurable

Success criteria that can be observed, counted, or otherwise evidenced — not vibes.

A

Achievable

Realistic within the resources, access, and ethics constraints of a candidature.

R

Relevant

Aligned with the DBA's dual mandate — contributing to practice and to knowledge.

T

Time-bound

Milestones and a completion horizon that discipline the work and keep it shippable.

The promise. The value of SMART is not the checklist — it's the discipline it forces: define your terms, name your evidence, bound your ambition, and commit to a timeline. That discipline is genuinely good for novice researchers.

02 · First-order critiques

The criteria themselves are a blunt tool

The most common critiques attack the content of the five criteria — how they constrain or misdirect research design. These are real, but they are surface-level: they ask whether the checklist is sensible.

F1First-order

Positivist / quantitative bias

"Measurable" presupposes findings that can be observed and verified objectively. Interpretive, qualitative, and practice-based designs sit uneasily with the framework.

F2First-order

Oversimplification

Complex, messy real-world problems get reduced to a checklist. The criteria themselves are vaguely defined — what counts as "measurable" or "achievable" is left to interpretation.

F3First-order

Rigidity & premature closure

Fixing scope too early discourages the emergent themes that often matter most in practice-based research — the discovery that happens along the way.

F4First-order

Instrumentality

Prioritises deliverables over the quality of the knowledge contribution — aims can become a tick-box exercise that satisfies supervisors rather than adds scholarly value.

F5First-order

Practitioner tension

DBA research is meant to blend practical and theoretical contribution. SMART's managerial framing can crowd out critical, reflexive theorising.

F6First-order

The achievability trap

Time-boxing is sensible, but "achievable" often gets traded against ambition — shrinking a contribution to what is convenient rather than what matters.

Where these land. Each of these is a fair critique of the criteria. But none of them explains why SMART misbehaves the way it does across entire cohorts of doctoral research. For that, you need the second-order view.

03 · Second-order challenges

What happens to the system when SMART is institutionalised

Second-order challenges ask a different question: not "are the criteria sensible?" but "what does the framework do to the research system that adopts it?" These are emergent, paradoxical effects — often the opposite of SMART's intent.

S1Second-order

Reactivity — the measure changes the measured

Performance measures are reactive (Espeland & Sauder): people change behaviour to hit the number. In doctoral completion research, four-year targets shifted topic choice toward safe, low-risk work; supervisors became project managers; theses were submitted incomplete. SMART objectives don't just describe research quality — they actively steer the DBA's content toward what is easy to measure.

S2Second-order

Goal displacement — means become the end

The objective stops being a proxy and becomes the goal (Merton). Aims get written for the audit, not for the knowledge. The viva, the progress review, the ethics form — each becomes a performance of the criteria, and the research is designed to satisfy the apparatus rather than answer the problem.

S3Second-order

Gaming & hollow compliance

Compliance with targets ranges on a continuum from acquiescence to gaming (Soin et al.). Students and supervisors produce "SMART-satisfying" proposals that are ceremonially checked and then quietly abandoned — proposals decoupled from what is actually done, eroding the very clarity SMART promised.

S4Second-order

Epistemic privilege — the silent filter

"Measurable" and "achievable" act as filters that de-legitimise other ways of knowing. DBA research is partly phronesis — practical wisdom that resists pre-specification. SMART quietly asserts that only testable knowledge counts, excluding valuable questions that cannot be ticked off.

S5Second-order

Temporal disciplining — the "T" is not neutral

Completion deadlines are a disciplinary technology. Framed within the neoTaylorist "projectification" of doctoral education, they push toward timely completion at the expense of care-inspired slowness — the slow, uncertain work that produces original insight. It decides which kinds of research are possible at all.

S6Second-order

The insider-research paradox

The DBA student is inside the organisation being studied — and their intervention changes it mid-study (double hermeneutic). A "specific, measurable, time-bound" target assumes a stable object of study; in practice the target is a moving one, so the framework conflicts with the reflexive, co-productive character of insider research.

S7Second-order

Managerialism's Trojan horse

SMART is a vehicle for the managerialist governance of doctoral education — reframing the PhD as "a problem to be managed". The effect lands on identity: the researcher becomes a project deliverer, the supervisor a project manager, and academics internalise institutional KPI pressure and police themselves.

S8Second-order

Relevance gets colonised

"Relevant" was meant to guarantee the DBA's practice contribution. Under managerialism it collapses into what the sponsor can measure — short-term and instrumental — crowding out longer-horizon contributions, critical theorising, and findings that unsettle managerial assumptions.

S9Second-order

Epistemic closure vs. genuine discovery

The deepest paradox: research begins with not-knowing. Pre-specified measurable objectives presuppose the outcome is knowable in advance — entrenching a confirmatory, hypothesis-testing default and marginalising inductive, emergent, and emancipatory designs.

S10Second-order

Aggregation — agenda-setting at field level

Individually a SMART objective is harmless. Collectively, thousands of risk-averse, measurable, achievable objectives set the field's research agenda — systematically filtering out hard, uncertain, unmeasurable questions and shaping what "DBA research" is over time.

One-sentence summary. First-order critiques say SMART is a blunt tool; second-order critiques say it is an active instrument — one that doesn't just fail to describe good research, but systematically deforms it: narrowing topics, hollowing out proposals, reshaping researcher identity, silencing phronesis, and substituting audit-compliance for knowledge contribution.

04 · The defence

Why SMART survives — as a framework

None of the above argues that SMART is worthless. The balanced position: SMART is good at what it is — a heuristic for project governance — and dangerous only when it is mistaken for the definition of good research. The line between the two is where the DBA candidate lives.

✓

It forces early precision. Novice researchers benefit from naming constructs, evidence, and scope before data collection — clarity they would otherwise never reach.

✓

It is audit-compatible. In an institutional system that demands defensible milestones, SMART gives you a legible record of what you set out to do.

✓

It is a contract. Written SMART aims align supervisor, researcher, and programme — managing expectations before they clash.

✓

It can be held loosely. Used as scaffolding — revised as the study unfolds — it supports emergence instead of blocking it.

The rule of thumb. Use SMART to make aims clear, bounded, and agreed. Never use it to make aims final. The moment a research aim stops being revisable, the research has stopped too.
